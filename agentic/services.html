A good job description attracts the right people and makes the decision easier. These are the practices we follow, and the template we start from.

  • Lead with the outcome, not a wish listOpen with what the person will achieve in their first six months. Candidates respond to the work, not to a long list of tools.
  • Separate must-have from nice-to-haveKeep must-haves to four or five real requirements. A long list of musts narrows the pool and hides the people who could grow into the role.
  • Write skills as things a person can showSay "built and run pipelines in production" instead of "knowledge of data". It is easier to test, and fairer to compare.
  • Use plain, inclusive languageAvoid jargon, slogans and gendered phrases. Say clearly what the working pattern, location and level of seniority are.
  • Describe the team and how work gets doneWho will they work with, how are decisions made, and what does a normal week look like?
  • Be clear about the processTell candidates the steps, who they will meet and how long it takes. A clear process is one of the strongest signs of a good employer.
  • Build the interview from the JDEvery must-have gets at least one question or exercise, with a simple scoring guide agreed before the first interview.
A job description that works
  1. Title and level A standard title and a clear level.
  2. The outcome What this person will achieve in six months.
  3. What they will do Four to six responsibilities, in plain words.
  4. Must have Four or five real requirements that can be shown.
  5. Nice to have A short list of extras that help.
  6. The team and how we work Who they join, how decisions are made.
  7. Working pattern Location, time zones and hours.
  8. The process The interview steps and how long it takes.
