Character design · for review

Meet the agents.

Seven characters, one per YPT service. They share one body so they read as a team, and each has its own colour, headgear and tool so you can tell who does what at a glance. Move your mouse: they watch you. Hover one: it smiles.

Expressions and sizes

One character, many uses.

The same drawing works as a large hero figure, a card illustration, and a small chat avatar. Expressions change with what the agent is doing.

How they're made

Drawn for YPT, owned by YPT.

These are original vector characters drawn in code for this site. They are not downloaded from a library, so there is no licence to check, no attribution to show, and no other company with the same mascots.

Original artwork

Each agent is built from the same parts: a shared body, plus its own colour, headgear and tool. New agents can be added in minutes.

Alive, not animated GIFs

They float, blink, wave their tool, follow the cursor with their eyes, and smile when you hover.

Tiny and sharp

Pure vector, a few kilobytes for the whole cast. Crisp on any screen, at any size, with no image files to load.

Still a static site

No backend and no external service. Everything runs in the visitor's browser from the same static files.

Options compared

Why not a free library?

I looked at the well-known free character sources. They are good, but each has a drawback for a company whose brand is its agents.

OptionSampleStrengthsDrawbacks for YPT

Unique to YPT. Each agent's role is visible in its design. Fully animated and interactive. Nothing to license. Any new pose or agent has to be drawn by us (quick, but not instant).

Free, instant, endless variations. Randomly generated, so a design says nothing about the agent's job. Used on many other websites. Static: no expressions or movement.
